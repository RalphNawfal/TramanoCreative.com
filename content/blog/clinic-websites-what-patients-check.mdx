Before booking a doctor in Lebanon, patients check five things: who the doctor is and how qualified they are, where the clinic is, when it's open, whether it works with their insurer, and how to book. A clinic website that answers those in that order, in plain text near the top of the page, does most of its job. Everything else, including the design, comes after. Our [clinic and medical websites](/clinic-website-design-lebanon/) page covers how we build them.

## Who is the doctor?

This is the question that decides the booking, so it goes first, not on an About page three clicks away.

Patients want to know:

- **Specialty,** stated plainly ("cardiologist", not "heart health solutions").
- **Qualifications** and where they were earned.
- **Hospital affiliations,** which many Lebanese patients treat as a key signal of standing.
- **Years in practice,** and any particular focus within the specialty.
- **A real photograph.** Patients want to recognise the person they're about to see.

All of this should be written as text on the page, not only in an image of a certificate. Text is what Google and AI assistants can read. When a patient asks "a good dermatologist in Achrafieh who speaks French", only a page that says those things in words can be the answer.

## Where is the clinic, and can I get there?

Location questions in Lebanon are about landmarks and parking as much as addresses:

- **The building and the landmark** people actually use to find it.
- **The floor,** and whether there's a lift.
- **Parking,** or the nearest place to park.
- **A map link** that opens directions on the patient's phone, with the pin in the right place.

The same details belong in the clinic's Google Business Profile, because that's what a patient's phone shows when they search the clinic's name on the way. If the website and the profile disagree, patients trust neither.

## When is it open?

Opening hours are the detail most likely to be wrong, because they change: holidays, the doctor's hospital days, summer hours. A patient who arrives at a closed door is unlikely to come back.

Keep one set of hours, on the website and the Google profile, and update both together. If the doctor sees patients at more than one clinic, list each location with its own days.

## Does the clinic work with my insurance?

For many patients this decides between two otherwise equal doctors, and it's often missing from clinic websites entirely.

List the insurers and third-party payers the clinic works with, and say plainly when a patient pays first and claims later. If the list changes, a short line saying "call to confirm your insurer" is better than an out-of-date list.

## How much is a consultation?

Not every clinic publishes its fee, but those that do remove one of the main reasons patients call before booking. If fees vary by visit type, a starting figure or a range, with what changes it, is enough.

## How do I book?

In Lebanon, most clinic bookings happen on WhatsApp or by phone, and the website should work with that rather than pretend otherwise.

The honest version is an **appointment request**: the patient chooses a reason for the visit and a preferred day, and the site sends that to the clinic by WhatsApp. The clinic replies with a confirmed time. The page says clearly that this is a request, and how quickly the clinic usually replies. [WhatsApp and your website](/blog/whatsapp-and-your-website/) covers how to build that message so it arrives complete.

Two things to avoid:

- **Showing a request as a confirmed booking,** when the site isn't connected to the clinic's calendar.
- **Asking for symptoms or medical history in the form.** Form contents pass through third-party services. Ask for the general reason for the visit; the details belong in the consultation.

## Does it work on a phone, on mobile data?

Most patients look a clinic up on their phone, often on mobile data. A site that takes ten seconds to load a photo carousel loses them before they see the doctor's name. Keep the first screen light: the doctor, the specialty, and the buttons to call or request an appointment. [Building for slow connections](/blog/building-for-slow-connections/) explains what that takes.

## What languages should it be in?

Whatever languages the doctor consults in, and patients need to know which those are. A line such as "Consultations in Arabic, French and English" is one of the most useful sentences a clinic page can carry. If the site itself is in more than one language, every page should exist in each, not just the homepage.
