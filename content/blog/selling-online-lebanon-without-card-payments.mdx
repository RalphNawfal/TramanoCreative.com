Most Lebanese businesses sell online without taking card payments at all. The order is placed on the website, sent to the shop on WhatsApp, and paid in cash on delivery or by a local money transfer app. Card processing is possible for some businesses, but approval is slow and often unavailable for small ones, so the shops that sell well here are built around the payment methods customers already use. Our [e-commerce websites in Lebanon](/ecommerce-website-design-lebanon/) page covers the build side.

This post is the practical version: which methods work, how to combine them, and what the website has to do so that none of it feels like a workaround.

## Why is it hard to take card payments online in Lebanon?

Three reasons, and they stack:

- **Local gateways are slow to get.** A payment gateway through a Lebanese bank means an application, a review and often a long wait. Many small businesses never get approved.
- **The big international processors don't serve Lebanese businesses.** Stripe doesn't support businesses registered in Lebanon, and that rules out most of the plug-in checkouts that website platforms rely on.
- **Customers don't expect it.** Many shoppers here would rather pay cash at the door than enter card details on an unfamiliar site. A card-only checkout loses those customers even when it works.

None of this means you can't sell online. It means the checkout has to end somewhere other than a card form.

## What payment methods actually work?

In rough order of how often they're used by small online shops:

1. **Cash on delivery.** The default, and the method most customers trust. The risk is a refused delivery, which a confirmation step reduces.
2. **Local money transfer apps.** Good for deposits, pre-orders and custom work, where you need commitment before you spend money. Payment is confirmed by hand.
3. **Bank transfer.** Fine for larger orders and business customers, too slow for a quick purchase.
4. **Card payments,** where you have them. Worth offering as one option, never as the only one.

Most successful shops offer the first two and let the customer choose.

## How does a website take orders without a payment gateway?

By doing everything a checkout does except taking the money.

The customer browses products, adds them to a cart, changes quantities and sees a running total, exactly like any online shop. At the end, instead of a card form, they enter their name, phone number, delivery area and notes. The website then composes the whole order into one message and opens WhatsApp with it ready to send.

You receive a complete, structured order in the app you already watch: items, quantities, total, address, phone. You confirm it, arrange delivery, and collect payment at the door or by transfer.

## What does the website have to get right?

The payment step is missing, so everything else has to be exact:

- **Prices that don't change at the door.** Show prices in the currency you'll collect, and if you quote in dollars, say so on every product.
- **Delivery cost before the order is sent.** Lebanon's delivery costs vary by area, and a surprise charge at the door is the fastest way to a refused order. Show the cost for the customer's area in the cart.
- **What happens next.** Tell the customer when you'll confirm, how they'll pay and roughly when the order arrives. A WhatsApp order with no reply for three hours feels like a lost order.
- **Stock that's honest.** If something is out of stock, take it off the page. Confirming an order and then cancelling it costs more trust than never showing the item.

## How do you reduce refused deliveries?

Cash on delivery has one real cost: the customer who isn't home or changes their mind. You can't remove it, but you can shrink it:

- **Confirm every order before dispatch,** by message or a short call. Most fake or impulsive orders fall away here.
- **Ask for a deposit** on large, custom or made-to-order items, by transfer app.
- **Keep a short list** of numbers that refused deliveries.

A WhatsApp-based order helps with all three, because every order arrives tied to a real phone number in a conversation you can continue.

## When is Shopify the right answer?

A hosted platform like Shopify is excellent when you have a working payment route, a large catalogue and enough orders to justify the monthly fee and the apps. We'll recommend it when that's the case.

Without card processing, though, Shopify's checkout is the one feature you can't use, and you'd be paying monthly for a shop that still ends in WhatsApp. For many Lebanese businesses, a lighter custom site that ends in a WhatsApp order does the same job, loads faster on mobile data and costs less over two years. ## Can you still advertise and measure sales?

Yes. You count the WhatsApp order as the conversion, because that's the moment the customer commits. What happens inside WhatsApp is invisible to analytics, so you reconcile the tracked orders against real deliveries once a month.
