When a Lebanese property listing gets views but no enquiries, the buyer arrived interested and left with a question the listing didn't answer. Usually it's one of five: the price, the exact area, the condition, practical details like parking and electricity, or how to get in touch quickly. Fixing those gaps does more than paying for more views, on a portal or on your own site. Our [real estate websites in Lebanon](/real-estate-website-design-lebanon/) page covers how we build agency sites around this.

## Is the price there?

"Price on request" is the most common reason a viewed listing gets no enquiry. Buyers don't read it as an invitation to call. They read it as "probably too expensive", or as a sign the price depends on who's asking. Either way, many move on.

If you can show the price, show it, in US dollars, which is how Lebanese property is quoted. If you can't, a range or a "from" figure still answers the question. The only buyers you lose by publishing a price are the ones who couldn't afford it anyway.

## Does the listing say exactly where it is?

"Beirut" or "Metn" isn't a location; it's a region. Buyers choose by neighbourhood, by street, by how close it is to a school, a road or their family. A vague location makes them do research before they can decide whether the property is even worth a call.

Name the neighbourhood and the landmarks people use to describe it. If you can't reveal the exact building before a viewing, say what you can: the area, the street side, how far from the main road. A map with an approximate area circle is better than nothing.

## Do the photos answer the questions, or just look nice?

Good property photos are a walk-through, not a highlight reel. Buyers want to see:

- **Every room,** including the kitchen and bathrooms, not only the living room at its best angle.
- **The view,** taken from the property, honestly.
- **The building entrance and lobby,** which say a lot about upkeep.
- **The parking** and the street outside.

Twenty to thirty photos is normal. Lead with the strongest one, order the rest as if walking through, and make sure they load quickly on a phone. A gallery that takes ten seconds on mobile data loses the buyer before the third photo.

## Are the practical details there?

These are the questions a Lebanese buyer will ask on the first call, so answer them in the listing:

- **Electricity:** hours of state power, generator subscription, solar.
- **Water:** supply and tank.
- **Parking:** how many spaces, covered or open.
- **Condition:** new, renovated, or needs work, and what's included.
- **Floor and lift,** and whether there's a generator for the lift.
- **Building fees** and what they cover.

Every one you answer is a reason not to skip your listing for one that did.

## How easy is it to enquire?

Count the steps between "I'm interested" and the agent knowing about it. A listing that needs a form with eight fields, or a call during office hours, loses buyers browsing at 11pm.

The quickest route here is WhatsApp, with the property reference already written into the message. The buyer taps, presses send, and the agent knows exactly which listing it is. [WhatsApp and your website](/blog/whatsapp-and-your-website/) covers how to build that link. It also tells you which listings and areas actually produce enquiries, which is the data you need to decide where to spend on promotion.

## Portals or your own site?

Both, because they do different jobs.

**Portals** bring buyers who have never heard of your agency. That's their strength, and it's worth paying for.

**Your own site** catches everyone else: buyers referred by friends, people who saw a listing on Instagram, people who searched your agency's name, and past clients. On a portal they're one click from a competitor's listing. On your site they aren't.

Your own site also keeps what portals throw away. When a property sells, a portal listing disappears. On your site, the page can stay, marked as sold, still ranking for the search that found it and pointing buyers at similar current listings. Over a few years, that's the difference between a site that brings enquiries and one that only describes the agency.

## What should you fix first?

In order of impact for most agencies:

1. **Show prices,** or at least ranges.
2. **Name the area** properly.
3. **Add the practical details** buyers always ask.
4. **Make the enquiry one tap** on WhatsApp, with the property named.
5. **Reshoot the weakest photos,** starting with your most-viewed listings.

None of these needs a new website, and all of them work on portals too.
