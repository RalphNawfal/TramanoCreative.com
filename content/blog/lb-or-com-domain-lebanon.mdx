For most Lebanese businesses, a **.com** is the practical choice: it's quick to register, recognised everywhere, and works well for customers abroad. A **.lb** clearly signals a Lebanese business but has stricter registration requirements. Whichever you choose, two things matter more than the ending: the domain must be registered in your own name, and the site behind it must be light enough to load fast on Lebanese connections. ## What's the difference between .lb and .com?

Both work the same way technically. The differences are practical:

- **.com** is the global default. Anyone can register one in minutes through any registrar. Customers in Lebanon, the Gulf and the diaspora all recognise it.
- **.lb** is Lebanon's country-code domain, managed by the Lebanese Domain Registry. It tells visitors and search engines that you're a Lebanese business. Registration has historically required supporting documents, such as a commercial registration or a trademark, so it takes more effort.

There's also **.com.lb**, a second-level option under the same registry, used by many commercial sites.

## Which one should you choose?

**Choose a .com if:**
- You sell to customers abroad, or plan to.
- You want the domain registered today, without paperwork.
- The .com version of your name is available.

**Choose a .lb if:**
- Your customers are almost entirely in Lebanon.
- Being visibly Lebanese is part of your brand.
- You have the documents the registry asks for.

**Register both if** you can. Point one at the other, so customers who type either reach you, and nobody else can take the version you don't use.

## Does the domain affect Google rankings?

A little, and less than people think. A country-code domain tells Google the site is aimed at Lebanon, which can help for local searches. But a .com sends the same signal in other ways: content written for Lebanese customers, a Lebanese phone number, prices in the currency people actually pay in, and a Google Business Profile. Those carry much more weight than the domain ending.

What matters far more for rankings is the site itself: speed, clear content and pages that answer what people search. [Technical SEO basics](/blog/technical-seo-basics/) covers what Google actually checks.

## Does the site need to be hosted in Lebanon?

Usually not. A common assumption is that a site for Lebanese customers must sit on a server in Lebanon to load quickly. In practice, a well-built site served through a global content network, with servers close to the region, loads quickly for Lebanese visitors.

What makes sites slow here is almost always the site, not the server: oversized images, page builders loading code the page doesn't use, and dozens of third-party scripts. A heavy site is slow wherever it's hosted. [Building for slow connections](/blog/building-for-slow-connections/) explains what actually makes the difference.

## Who should own the domain and hosting accounts?

You should. This is the most important point in this post, and the most often ignored.

We regularly meet businesses whose domain was registered by a former agency, a freelancer or an employee, in that person's name, on their account. Everything works until the relationship ends. Then renewing the domain, moving the site or even changing the email settings depends on someone who may no longer answer.

The rule is simple:

- **The domain is registered in the business owner's name,** on an account the owner controls, with an email address the business will always have.
- **The hosting account is the business's,** too, where possible.
- **Agencies and developers get access,** not ownership.

If your domain is currently in someone else's name, ask them to transfer it to you now, while relations are good. It's a short process when everyone cooperates, and a very long one when they don't.

## What happens if a domain expires?

The website and usually the email go down together, because both depend on the domain. If it stays unpaid past the grace period, it can be released and bought by someone else, including people who buy expired domains to resell them.

Turn on auto-renewal, keep the payment card current, and make sure renewal reminders go to an address someone actually reads. Lebanese cards are sometimes refused for international renewals, so check that the payment method works before the renewal date rather than on it.

## What should you have written down?

Keep a short record, somewhere the business owns:

- **Where the domain is registered** and the renewal date.
- **Where the site is hosted.**
- **Who has access** to each account.
- **Where email is managed.**

It takes ten minutes and saves days the first time something goes wrong.
